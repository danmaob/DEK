# Requirements Elicitation

Turning a vague ask into a clear, testable requirement.

## When to use
At the start of any new feature, project, or client request, before
any design or estimation happens.

## Approach
1. Restate the problem in one or two sentences and confirm it back —
   most misunderstandings happen here, not later.
2. Separate **goals** (why this matters), **constraints** (budget,
   deadline, existing systems it must fit), and **non-goals**
   (explicitly out of scope) into three short lists.
3. Ask about the current process/workaround, if any — it reveals
   implicit requirements the client won't think to state.
4. Identify who is affected (roles/personas) and what changes for each.
5. Flag ambiguity explicitly rather than guessing — a numbered list of
   open questions is a valid, useful output on its own.

## Anti-patterns
- Writing a requirements doc that is really a design doc in disguise
  (jumping to "how" before "what"/"why" is settled).
- Treating a single stakeholder's request as the full requirement
  without checking who else is affected.
- Silently resolving ambiguity with an assumption instead of stating it.

## Output
A short requirements note: problem, goals, constraints, non-goals,
affected roles, open questions.
